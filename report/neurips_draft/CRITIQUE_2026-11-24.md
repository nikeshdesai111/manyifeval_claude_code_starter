# Critique of the 23 November NeurIPS draft (`paper.pdf` from PRs #7 and #8)

Written 2026-11-24, before the revision in the same PR. Read as a hostile NeurIPS reviewer would, and against the
course rules: a *complete* draft in NeurIPS format, main paper at most 8 pages, references and appendices free.
Sources checked: `paper.tex`, `results/v2_s3/SUMMARY.md` and tables, `docs/pilot_report_betty.md`,
`docs/decision_tracker.md`, the agreed evaluation and reporting plan of 2026-11-23 ("complete evaluation, analysis,
and results-reporting plan"), and the team's related-work corrections document (NEED.pdf, 23 Nov 2026).

Severity: **[Fatal]** would sink the paper or the grade; **[Major]** a reviewer would cite it as a reason to reject;
**[Minor]** polish. Each item ends with what the revision does about it.

---

## 1. Fatal

**F1. It is not a complete draft.** The professor asked for a complete draft of the final report. The submitted
draft has a Results section that is one empty table and a red TODO, a Conclusion that is a TODO, and no discussion
of findings. It reads as a pre-registration, not a paper. Even without test numbers, a complete draft should show
every final exhibit at its final size, a written results narrative with the numbers left blank, and a discussion that
commits in advance to how each outcome will be read.
*Revision:* every main-paper exhibit in the agreed plan now has a sized placeholder (Tables 2 and 3, Figure 2, the
Evaluation 2 skill table), the results text is written with blanks, and the interpretation of each possible outcome is
fixed in Section 7 ("Reading the outcomes") before the numbers arrive. Training-side facts that exist (layer selection, extraction checks,
sparse-head fallbacks, the cross-generator diagnostic) are used.

**F2. The draft contradicts the agreed evaluation plan.** The 23 November plan fixes the paper's exhibits; the draft
predates or ignores it:
- *Test set.* The plan evaluates the full 456-configuration design on the 22 test groups: 30,096 prompts and 115,236
  prompt-rule rows per generator. The draft's test set is the 16,236 original prompts, with the expansion as a side
  table, and its Table 1 adds them up as if they were different things.
- *Evaluation 1 tables.* The plan's Table 2 (Objective 1) shows **all seven** comparisons with NLL reduction
  [95% CI], AUROC gain [95% CI] and types improved; Table 3 (Objective 2) shows RMSE reduction and Spearman gain. The
  draft shows only the four primary comparisons, uses MAE instead of Spearman, and has no AUROC interval.
- *Sign convention.* The plan writes every contrast as comparator → candidate with positive favouring the candidate.
  The draft uses "right minus left", which is the same sign but the opposite reading order, so the two documents will
  disagree in every caption.
- *Evaluation 2 is missing.* The plan makes the controlled-factor analysis (position, strict append, strict companion
  replacement, task context) a main-paper component: observed-effect figures with noise-adjusted RMS sensitivity and
  signed effects, plus one shared predictor-skill table scored by paired Δ-MSE. The draft has one paragraph, scores
  "the accuracy of its sign on pairs whose observed change is reliably non-zero", and the plan explicitly rejects that
  metric because filtering on observed significance selects noisy, large differences.
*Revision:* Sections 4, 6 and the appendix now follow the plan's estimands, exhibits, weights and sign convention.

**F3. Statements about the status of the study are now false.**
- "Generation for the expansion is under way" — it finished on 23 November (277,200 new responses per generator).
- "No feature, label or prediction for a test group has been computed or read" and "stay sealed until the analysis
  plan is frozen" — the owner authorised Objective 1 test scoring on 24 November and test features are being
  extracted. Objective 2 test scoring is not yet authorised.
- "On a 100-prompt check the two [Transformers and the generation engine] produced identical tokens and hidden states
  with cosine similarity of at least 0.998." The check in `docs/pilot_report_betty.md` compared the Mac (MPS) and CUDA
  *feature* backends, both Hugging Face Transformers; nothing compared hidden states with vLLM, whose numerics neither
  feature backend reproduces. The sentence claims a check that was not run.
*Revision:* all three corrected.

## 2. Major: framing and related work

**M1. The gap paragraph of the Introduction is wrong on all three counts** (NEED D1–D3). (i) "Labels usually come
from one sampled response per prompt": Heo et al. label one *greedy* response, while Lugoloobi et al. (5 or 50
rollouts), Expected Reward Prediction (32 samples) and Binder et al. all sample repeatedly. (ii) "Prompts are
collected rather than designed": Heo et al.'s IFEval-simple is controlled, and MOSAIC varies constraint type, count
and position. (iii) "Probes are compared with weak baselines": Ashok & May compare with a fine-tuned BERT-large that
reads the prompt and match or beat it in all but one setting. A reviewer who knows any of these papers stops reading
here. *Revision:* the Introduction credits pre-generation prediction (Heo; Ashok & May), sampled targets (Lugoloobi)
and constraint-level attention (SAT Probe), and states one open question.

**M2. Related Work misreports or omits the closest papers.**
- Rocchetti & Ferrara are cited as showing the constraint signal "is weak before generation and grows during it". Their
  temporal analysis probes fixed responses with negatives swapped in from other prompts, so the label cannot be
  predicted before the response by construction. It is not evidence about pre-generation forecasting (D4, R3).
- Hewitt & Liang (2019) is cited as the caveat that matched text comparators "address". Their caveat is probe
  expressivity, which a fixed linear head addresses; "what does a representation add beyond a baseline" is
  conditional probing, Hewitt et al. (2021), which is not cited (D5, E3).
- "Labels are probabilities from 20 samples" is listed as a difference from prior work; it is not (D6).
- SAT Probe's construction, including the maximum over constraint tokens, is presented as our design choice
  ("we take the maximum ... because of sink tokens"). It is theirs and must be credited next to Eq. 2 (F6).
- Heo et al. (2025b) is summarised as "approach chance in their hardest setting"; the checked wording is that internal
  representations help in some settings but remain unreliable for subtle violations.
- Missing: Lugoloobi et al. (pre-generation probes for expected success from rollouts — the closest work),
  Expected Reward Prediction (prompt-only prediction of a model's mean reward, which is the reason a text comparator
  is needed), Binder et al. (self vs other prediction, the precedent for the cross-generator control), Hewitt et al.
  (2021), MOSAIC and CSE (controlled multi-constraint studies), Future Lens, and the deployment-scope papers (VerIFY,
  CONPACT, MultiChallenge, Instruction Hierarchy).
- "Our study differs in four ways" claims firsts that each have a precedent (F1 in NEED).
*Revision:* Section 2 is rewritten from NEED §4, with every correction applied, and the novelty is stated as the
research setting, not as a single new method.

**M3. The paper's central question and its primary comparisons do not match.** The Introduction says "the question
that matters is the incremental one" (what internal states add), but the four pre-registered primary comparisons are
substitutions (H vs P, A vs P, H vs A); the additions (5–7) are secondary. A reviewer will say the primary tests do
not answer the stated question. *Revision:* the question is stated as two families (substitution: which
representation is more useful; conditional addition: what internal features add to a fixed text predictor, after
Hewitt et al. 2021) with the priority order taken from the amendment, and the paper says which family each claim rests
on.

**M4. "They differ only in how the prompt is represented" is an overclaim.** H is one 4,096-d layer chosen by a
five-way search; P is a 1,024-d frozen encoder with no search; A is 1,024 or 1,152 values over all layers and heads.
Dimension, source and search budget all differ. The draft discloses the layer search in one place but repeats the
"only" claim in the abstract and Section 5. *Revision:* the claim is narrowed to "same head, structure block, labels,
penalty grid and selection rule", and the remaining differences are listed where the comparison is defined.

**M5. The cross-generator control is oversold and its likely outcome is ignored.** The draft says the control "asks
whether the signal is specific to the model that writes the response" and helps "address the probing caveat". On
training CV the other generator's hidden states already match or beat the generator's own in two of four cells
(Llama Objective 1: 0.397 vs 0.414 log loss; Qwen Objective 2: 11.61 vs 12.19 MSE ×10³). The reference is also not one
of the seven pre-specified comparisons, has no interval in the plan, and got its own layer search. *Revision:* it is
described as a reference inspired by Binder et al., a gain from own features is read as a conditional predictive gain
and not introspection, and the CV diagnostic is disclosed so the reader is not surprised.

**M6. "Strong text encoder" is asserted, not shown.** On training CV, B1 (structure + frozen BGE-M3 prompt embedding)
improves on structure alone by only 0.021 (Llama) and 0.016 (Qwen) log loss, far less than the hidden state. A frozen
general-purpose sentence embedding with a linear head is a reasonable but not strong reader of the prompt; Ashok & May
use a fine-tuned BERT-large. *Revision:* "strong" removed; the limitation says a fine-tuned prompt reader could narrow
any gap.

## 3. Major: methods and statistics

**S1. Absolute NLL is uninterpretable without a reference.** NLL against 20 Bernoulli outcomes contains the
irreducible binomial entropy, so a reader cannot tell whether a 0.01 reduction is large. Paired differences are fine,
but the paper needs anchor rows. *Revision:* Table 3's caption states the NLL unit and scale, and the planned appendix reports the structure-only
and other-generator references next to every predictor (training-CV values already in Appendix H). A training-mean
reference for Objective 1 is not in the agreed plan and was not added.

**S2. Unadjusted intervals on 28 headline cells.** Four primary comparisons × two objectives × two generators, plus
three secondary, all with pointwise 95% intervals. That is the agreed policy, but the draft never says what a reader
should conclude if one of 16 primary intervals excludes zero. *Revision:* Section 6.1 states that a single interval is not read as confirmation or its absence as equivalence,
and Section 7 says results that differ in sign between generators are reported as generator-specific. (A "both
generators must agree" decision rule was drafted and then removed after review, because it is not in the agreed plan.)

**S3. Twenty-two clusters.** All inference rests on 22 groups. Percentile bootstraps with 22 clusters under-cover. The
plan adds a t(21) interval and leave-one-group-out as sensitivity; the draft mentions only a jackknife. *Revision:*
both sensitivity analyses named.

**S4. Earlier exposure of test groups is under-weighted.** 16 of the 22 test groups were training groups under the
earlier split and shaped earlier choices (penalty grids, layer candidates, the decision to retire MLP heads). The
draft says the final models were refitted without them, which is true but does not remove the selection effect.
*Revision:* stated plainly in Limitations, without drama.

**S5. Objective 2 duplicates Objective 1 at k = 1** and is close to the mean of Objective 1 predictions elsewhere; the
draft does not say why Objective 2 is not redundant. *Revision:* one sentence on why a prompt-level head can differ
(joint structure, equal prompt weights) and the averaged-Objective-1 reference row.

**S6. Evaluation 2 estimands are not defined in the draft.** Noise-adjusted RMS sensitivity, strict matching, cell
weights and Δ-MSE are central to the plan and absent from the paper. *Revision:* defined compactly in Section 6.1 with
the full weights in the appendix.

## 4. Major: page budget

**P1. The draft has no room for its own results.** The main text ends mid-page 8 with one empty table. The agreed
plan's main-paper exhibits (inventory table, two Evaluation 1 tables, four two-panel observed-effect figures, one
predictor-skill table) cannot fit in the remaining ~25 lines. Something has to move, and the draft does not say what.
*Revision:* text was cut (the overview figure moved to Appendix A, tuning and feature details to the appendix,
related work kept to about 800 words), the plan's Tables 2 and 3 share one table body, and the four Evaluation 2
observed-effect figures are combined into one main figure drawn at final size (empty axes): the noise-adjusted RMS
panels by target type plus the append panel, with the signed heatmaps moved to the appendix. The README records this layout decision so
the authors can overrule it.

## 5. Minor

- Title is fine; the abstract is long (≈260 words) and ends on "test results are pending", which is the weakest
  possible last sentence for a reviewer.
- "Penalised logistic-type regression" — say "ridge-penalised logistic regression".
- Mixed British/American spelling (penalised/normalization). The revision uses one convention throughout.
- Figure 1 boxes say "Sealed test" and "Test expansion", both outdated.
- Table 1 caption calls prompt-rule rows "the units of Objective 1", but the NLL unit is the response × rule outcome.
- The reference list lacks ten papers the corrected text needs; the KEEN Anthology ID was already correct in the bib.
- Several 2025–2026 citations are arXiv-only and must stay labelled as preprints. Lugoloobi et al. is cited as COLM
  2026, as NEED says (the arXiv record notes the acceptance; an earlier version was an ICLR 2026 workshop poster).
- Appendix G (history; now Appendix I) should date each change so a grader can follow the pre-registration story.

## 6. Points in NEED.pdf that the revision does not adopt, and why

- **"Comparisons 6 and 7 are primary" (NEED E3).** `CLAUDE.md` and the 2026-11-22 priorities make 1, 2, 3 and 4
  primary and 5–7 secondary, and the evaluation plan uses the same split. Kept as secondary; flagged for Nicky.
- **"B2-FT (LoRA BGE-M3) is reported as a secondary reference row" (NEED F4).** V2 retired the LoRA encoder and no
  S3 model exists, so the paper does not claim it. The limitation about a fine-tuned prompt reader stays.
- **"Replicate on the 29 IFTrain types" (NEED E8, F14).** The IFBench work in progress on its own branch uses the
  IFBench *test* inventory (42 candidate types after review), not IFTrain, and has no results. The paper mentions an
  IFBench replication only as future work, without a type count. Flagged for Nicky.
- **CSE's pairwise co-failure as motivation for a "where the multi-rule gain comes from" analysis (NEED §5).** NEED
  marks this as not yet in the decided plan, so it is cited in Related Work only.

## 7. Independent review of the revision (2026-11-24)

A separate reviewer agent checked the revision against the repository, NEED.pdf, the evaluation plan and the cited
sources. It found one blocker (the paper said no test feature was extracted before the refit; features for the
original test prompts were extracted with the training prompts in mid-November), seven major issues (pre-registration
wording stronger than the record, a decision rule not in the plan, tables that would overflow with real numbers, a
figure placeholder far smaller than the planned figure, a "score an existing response" overstatement inherited from
NEED, the cross-generator reference mislabelled as a conditional gain, and an undisclosed training-CV result that the
averaged Objective 1 predictions beat the dedicated Objective 2 heads) and thirteen minor ones (including the Lugoloobi
venue, two author orders, notation for the addition predictors, the recipe hash, and the backend-check wording). All
were fixed in the same PR, and a second review pass confirmed the fixes, except: the paper checklist of the official template is not included (course draft), and
an original-vs-expansion sensitivity analysis is mentioned as a limitation but is not in the agreed plan.

The second pass also asked that the 24 Nov test authorisation, the evaluation plan (with a hash) and an S3
pre-specification revision be committed to the repository; the paper's claims about them rest on the project
threads until then. The paper no longer claims the synthetic estimator check, which is not yet recorded in the repo.
